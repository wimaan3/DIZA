# Welfare checks and "who can't evacuate": evidence for DIZA's single problem

- **Session:** cs1-retell (research subagent), written 2026-10-17. Not committed.
- **Question:** Do 911 in a disaster and senior-living communities on an ordinary day share one problem: (a) relatives asking "Is my mother OK? Has she been evacuated?" and (b) knowing which vulnerable people still need help to leave?
- **Builds on, and does not repeat:** `OPERATOR_WORKFLOW.md` (LAPD AAR p.15 summary, the Eaton alert timeline, the Mitchell 911 calls in one line, language-line timings, all-ages LEP) and `EXISTING_INFRASTRUCTURE.md` (211 LA, Alert LA County, the welfare-check channels). This file adds the details behind those lines and the parts they don't cover.
- **Tags:** **[V]** primary (government document, statute, peer-reviewed paper, utility regulatory filing, or our own calculation from Census data). **[S]** secondary (news or vendor). **[UNVERIFIED]** not confirmed. **[EST]** our estimate.

## Summary

1. **The people who died were the people who couldn't leave.** The Eaton and Palisades fires killed 31 people: 19 in Eaton and 12 in Palisades **[V McChrystal AAR p.27]**. The LA Times found the **median age of the dead was 77** and that **at least a third had impairments that could affect mobility** **[S]**. The CA Attorney General gives an average age of 77 for the Eaton dead **[V]**. 18 of the 19 Eaton deaths were west of Lake Ave **[S]**.
2. **Someone did know.** Anthony Mitchell Sr. (68, amputee) and his son Justin (35, cerebral palsy) died in west Altadena. Their usual caretaker was in hospital that night. They made **three 911 calls** (two from Mitchell Sr., one from a relative) saying "two disabled people in the house." The county's paratransit service, AccessLA, says it was ready by 4 a.m. and was never called **[S LAist, from county 911 recordings]**.
3. **Relatives' calls flood the system and mostly concern people who are already safe:**
   - **Palisades:** LAPD logged 160+ evacuation-assistance calls in a manual "master incident." The first calls were about elderly people, then *"family, friends, and neighbors who could not reach their loved ones"*. Many concerned *"individuals who had been successfully evacuated"* **[V LAPD AAR pp.15, 53]**.
   - **Hurricane Helene:** in Buncombe County, **11,000+ welfare-check requests** led to **150 households (about 1.4%)** being flagged for an immediate check, *"particularly those with medical concerns"* **[S]**.
   - **Maui:** more than **3,000** people reported unaccounted for were later found safe. The death toll was 102 **[S]**.
   - **Camp Fire:** the missing list peaked at **1,276**, swollen by a backlog of early 911 calls and duplicates. 85 people died **[S]**.
4. **Facilities also lose track of residents:**
   - **OIG (2007–10 disasters):** 19 of 24 nursing-home plans did not send next-of-kin contacts with evacuated residents. One home's residents went to 20 host facilities, and four homes temporarily "lost" residents **[V]**.
   - **Eaton, Altadena:** MonteCedro left 2 residents behind, one of them 100 years old **[S]**.
   - **Eaton, Pasadena:** The Terraces at Park Marino left a wheelchair user for hours. She was rescued after her **"responsible party" called police**. That was a welfare-check call that worked **[S]**.
   - **LAist:** more than 90% of the 70+ evacuated assisted-living plans it reviewed were out of date **[S]**.
   - **Tubbs Fire 2017:** staff left 20+ residents at Villa Capri, and **families and responders evacuated them** **[S]**.
5. **The rules require tracking but not proactive family updates:**
   - **Nursing homes (CMS 42 CFR 483.73):** must track sheltered and relocated residents. They must have a way to give out residents' "general condition and location" and to report occupancy and needs to the authority having jurisdiction. They are **not** required to push status to families **[V]**.
   - **California assisted living (RCFEs, H&S Code §1569.695):** must confirm each resident's location, have a communication process with families, and identify residents with special needs **[V]**.
   - **No law requires a live, shared status list across facilities** **[V, by absence]**.
6. **Registries don't solve "who can't leave":**
   - LA County dropped its SNAP disability registry around 2016, citing low uptake and limited use **[S]**. The 2025 feasibility study's outcome is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
   - In Harvey, fewer than 5% of Texas registrants were contacted **[S, citing NCD]**.
   - SCE has about **121,000 Medical Baseline accounts** but about **1.8M customers flagged as having access or functional needs** **[V CPUC filing]**.
   - LA County has about **341,000 adults 65+ with an ambulatory difficulty** **[V ACS calc]**.
7. **Language:** **36.4% of LA County residents 65+ (572,879 of 1,575,175) speak English less than "very well"**. Among older Spanish speakers the share is 71%, and among Asian/Pacific Island language speakers 70% **[V ACS 2024 1-yr, our calculation]**.
8. **Everyday senior-living load is the weakest link.** No independent study measures staff time spent on family check-in calls. The only figures come from vendors ("40% of caregiver time", "40 calls a day") **[S vendor, unsourced]**.
9. **Verdict (detail in §7):** the disaster half is well evidenced. The overlap with senior living is real, but the deaths were mostly in **private homes**, not facilities. Pitch it as **"the people a household or facility already knows can't leave, plus the relatives calling about them"**, not as a senior-living product.

## 1. Eaton and Palisades 2025: who died, where, and whether anyone knew

### Counts
- **Totals:** Palisades 12 dead. Eaton *"at least 19 confirmed deaths."* **[V]** ([McChrystal AAR, LA County, Sept 2025, p.27](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/bos/supdocs/207915.pdf))
- **West of Lake:** 18 of the 19 Eaton deaths were west of Lake Ave. The one exception was on Boston St, east of Lake, found later. **[S]** ([LAist on Citygate](https://laist.com/brief/news/climate-environment/new-report-on-eaton-fire-evacuation-alerts-finds-no-failure-by-officials))
- **Earlier counts:** 17 of 17 were west of Lake. 15 died where the first order came at 3:25 a.m., and 2 where it came at 5:42 a.m. **[S]** ([LA Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/17-deaths-eaton-fire-were-024433120.html))

### Ages and disability
- **LA Times (2 Apr 2025):** *"the median age of those killed was 77, and at least a third of them suffered impairments that could affect their mobility."* **[S]** (LA Times analysis, Jenny Jarvie, [via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/botched-fire-evacuations-l-county-161140646.html))
  - The methodology isn't published. The analysis was done when the toll was about 28–29. **[UNVERIFIED: whether it covers the final 31]**
- **CA Attorney General (12 Feb 2026):** opened a civil-rights investigation into West Altadena. *"The average age of those who died was 77"*. It examines possible **race, age or disability** discrimination and *"whether the systems and structures at play contributed to a delay in the County's evacuation notice."* **[V]** ([oag.ca.gov](https://oag.ca.gov/node/618579))
- **Eaton age range:** 32–95 per medical examiner data, with most victims over 65. **[S]** ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/california-fire-victims-los-angeles-altadena-eaton-fire/))
- **Palisades victims named early:** Charles Mortimer (84), Betty O'Meara (94), Annette Rossilli (85), Mark Shterenberg (80), Arthur Simoneau (69), Jeffrey Takeyama (69) and Dianna Webb (82). Rory Sykes (32, cerebral palsy) died in Malibu; his mother said he refused to leave. **[S]** ([Fox LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/palisades-eaton-fire-victims-california-wildfires), [ABC7](https://abc7.com/post/what-know-killed-los-angeles-wildfires/15791281/))
- **Context:** about 21% of residents in the Eaton zone and 26% in the Palisades zone are over 65. **[S]** ([CBS News analysis](https://cbsnews.com/amp/news/elderly-los-angeles-wildfire-victims))
- **Official AAR on vulnerable residents:** *"Vulnerable populations, especially older residents who were not monitoring alerts due to a digital divide and possible mobility challenges, faced increased risks of delayed evacuation"* (p.63). *"Vulnerable groups, including the elderly and non-ambulatory individuals, often relied on caregivers, family members, or neighbors for evacuation alerts. Many older residents remained unaware of the danger during the early hours of January 8"* (p.64). **[V]** ([McChrystal AAR](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/bos/supdocs/207915.pdf))

### Did anyone know they needed help?
- **The Mitchells:** see summary point 2. Further details:
  - **Who:** Anthony Mitchell Sr., 68, who used a wheelchair, walker or cane after losing a leg, and Justin, 35, paraplegic with cerebral palsy, who needed 2–3 people to get out of bed.
  - **Where:** 100 block of Terrace St, west of Lake.
  - **Why alone:** the usual caretaker, son Jordan, was in hospital after a fall.
  - **Calls:** 6:03 a.m. (*"There's two disabled people in the house"*), 6:14 a.m. (*"My house is on fire … Hurry, please"*), and 6:35 a.m. from a family member (*"They can't get out of the house"*). At 6:38 a.m. dispatch radioed *"unable to get out, sees flames nearby."*
  - **AccessLA:** the county's paratransit service said it was ready by 4 a.m. and was not called.
  - **Sources:** county fire 911 recordings released to LAist after 5 months. **[S, based on primary recordings]** ([LAist, 14 Jul 2025](https://laist.com/brief/news/climate-environment/despite-three-911-calls-two-homebound-disabled-men-died-in-the-eaton-fire-waiting-for-rescue))
  - **Answer to "did anyone know?":** the household and the family knew. 911 knew 2.5 hours after the evacuation order. No county list had them. Their son said a registry *"would have saved my father."* **[S]** (LA Times via Yahoo, above)
- **The state had warned the county.** Cal OES had told the county it must do more to *"adequately address"* evacuation planning for vulnerable residents, less than 16 months before the fires. The Times reported the county did not follow those recommendations. **[S]** (LA Times via Yahoo, above). The Cal OES document itself was **[UNVERIFIED]** (not retrieved).
- **Competing official finding:** Citygate, hired by LA County Fire, found **no failure or delay** in evacuation orders west of Lake. It also says resources were *"appropriately triaged to perform evacuations, rescues, and structure protection of assisted living centers and homes where many seniors and people with disabilities lived."* **[V]** ([Citygate, May 2026, §1](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1208708_InvestigationReportoftheEatonFireEvacuationAlerts_05-18-26_.pdf))

### Senior facilities in the Eaton fire (they did not account for the deaths)
- **County AAR:** LASD and LACoFD evacuated Pasadena Park Healthcare within the first hour and **MonteCedro from about 4:00 a.m.** using Pasadena transit buses. **[V]** (McChrystal p.63)
- **Citygate on MonteCedro:** the evacuation *"took several hours due to the number of senior residents, many with limited mobility having to be transported down flights of stairs."* **[V]** (Citygate p.42)
- **No Eaton or Palisades death has been reported inside a licensed facility.** **[UNVERIFIED by absence]** The deaths were in private homes.

## 3. Senior living and nursing homes in disasters

### Failures
- **OIG 2012** (*Gaps Continue To Exist in Nursing Home Emergency Preparedness and Response During Disasters: 2007–2010*, OEI-06-09-00270), from site visits to 24 nursing homes after floods, hurricanes and wildfires. **[V]** ([PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/oei/reports/oei-06-09-00270.pdf))
  - **p.13, Table 6:** **19 of 24** plans did not send *"name and contact information for next of kin/power of attorney"* with residents. 10 of 24 had no *"method to account for individuals during and after evacuation"*. 7 of 24 had no way to identify residents (wristband or nametag).
  - **p.15, Table 9:** 4 of 24 plans did not include notifying resident families. 7 of 24 had no backup for telephone failure. 16 of 24 had not worked with emergency managers on the plan.
  - **p.17:** *"residents of 1 home were sent to 20 host facilities."* Four evacuated homes reported *"temporarily 'losing' residents"* and trouble matching residents to their wheelchairs.
  - **p.19:** local LTC ombudsmen *"were often unable to contact or visit residents."*
- **Tubbs Fire 2017, Santa Rosa:**
  - **Staffing:** Villa Capri had 4 overnight staff for 62 residents.
  - **What happened:** CDSS found staff left 20+ residents. **Family members and emergency responders evacuated them.**
  - **Outcome:** CDSS moved to revoke both Oakmont licenses. Oakmont admitted *"staff departed Villa Capri without all residents being evacuated"* and settled on probation.
  - **Law:** this led to AB 3098 (2018).
  - **[S]** ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/santa-rosa-assisted-living-facilities-could-lose-licenses-after-dss-north-bay-fires-investigation/4165881/), [ABC7 settlement](https://abc7news.com/senior-living-center-reaches-settlement-after-deadly-north-bay-fires/4727697/), [Assembly analysis of AB 3098](https://altc.assembly.ca.gov/sites/altc.assembly.ca.gov/files/AB%203098.pdf))
- **Eaton 2025, MonteCedro (Altadena, CDSS-licensed):**
  - Two independent-living residents were missed in two sweeps, including a **100-year-old** found on the 3rd floor at about 9:30 a.m.
  - CDSS cited the facility for not following its own evacuation procedures. The operator is appealing.
  - **[S]** ([LA Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/dont-lose-deputies-describe-finding-021144490.html), [CBS LA](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/losangeles/news/altadena-senior-living-centert-proper-eaton-fire-evacuation-procedures-state-report))
- **Eaton 2025, The Terraces at Park Marino (Pasadena):**
  - A wheelchair user in room 326 was left for hours.
  - The state report says her **"responsible party" called Pasadena police**, and firefighters rescued her.
  - Another son, Jim Benesh, *"called his mother repeatedly and tried to call The Terraces directly"* before driving there himself.
  - Cited in August 2025, under appeal.
  - **[S]** ([LAist, 7 Jan 2026](https://laist.com/news/climate-environment/eaton-fire-assisted-living-terraces-evacuation))
- **LAist records review:** of the **70+ assisted-living facilities evacuated** in January 2025, **more than 90% of emergency plans were out of date** and **more than a third were last approved 10 or more years ago**, despite the annual requirement.
  - The Terraces' plan named two relocation sites that were themselves unusable.
  - County ombudsman Rachel Tate: *"I don't think that facilities in Los Angeles County were braced the way they should be for regional incidents."*
  - **[S]** (same LAist article; [FIJ summary](https://fij.org/grantee-investigates-poor-emergency-planning-at-los-angeles-nursing-homes-in-wake-of-altadena-fire/))

### Federal rule: CMS Emergency Preparedness, 42 CFR 483.73 (nursing homes / SNFs and NFs only)
Source: [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483/subpart-B/section-483.73) **[V]**

| Provision | Text (abridged) | What it means for DIZA |
|---|---|---|
| (b)(2) | *"A system to track the location of on-duty staff and sheltered residents … during and after an emergency. If … relocated … document the specific name and location of the receiving facility"* | **Tracking is required.** The data exists, but how it's kept is unspecified (often paper) |
| (c)(5) | A means, in an evacuation, to release resident information *as permitted under 45 CFR 164.510(b)(1)(ii)* (HIPAA disaster disclosure) | Sharing with family and relief agencies is allowed |
| (c)(6) | *"A means of providing information about the general condition and location of residents … as permitted under 45 CFR 164.510(b)(4)"* | **Facilities must be able to answer "Is my mother OK, where is she?"** |
| (c)(7) | Provide *"occupancy, needs, and its ability to provide assistance"* to the authority having jurisdiction or the Incident Command Center | Facilities must report status to officials |
| (c)(8) | *"A method for sharing information from the emergency plan … with residents and their families or representatives"* | Families get the plan in advance. There is **no mandate to push real-time updates** |

- **Scope:** 483.73 covers Medicare/Medicaid long-term care facilities. It does **not** cover California assisted living (RCFEs), which are state-licensed by CDSS.

### California assisted living (RCFEs)
- **H&S Code §1569.695**, amended by AB 3098, in effect 1 Jan 2019. **[V statute; text via [FindLaw mirror](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/health-and-safety-code/hsc-sect-1569-695/)]**
  - **(a)(7)(C):** *"Process for communicating with residents, families, hospice providers, and others, as appropriate"*. Residents and responsible parties *"shall be informed of the process for communicating during an emergency."*
  - **(a)(7)(F):** operation of powered assistive devices *"including … oxygen equipment and wheelchairs."*
  - **(a)(7)(G):** *"A process for identifying residents with special needs, such as hospice, and a plan for meeting those needs."*
  - **(a)(7)(H):** *"Procedures for confirming the location of each resident during an emergency response."*
  - **(g):** the plan must be available to local emergency responders.
  - Plans must be updated and approved annually. Staff must be trained at hire and annually, with quarterly drills on every shift.
- **22 CCR §87211 (reporting):**
  - Catastrophes must be reported to licensing (and the health officer, when appropriate) within 24 hours.
  - Fires go to the fire authority immediately and to licensing by the next working day.
  - An *"unexplained absence of any resident"* goes to licensing **and the responsible person** within 7 days.
  - **No real-time status reporting to families or the incident command is required.** **[V]** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/22-CCR-87211))
- **SB 582 (Ch. 546, Stats. 2025):** *encourages* RCFEs to share their emergency plan with the county Medical Health Operational Area Coordinator. It is encouraged, not mandated. **[S]** ([Digital Democracy](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260sb582))

**Bottom line for §3:**
- Both regimes require facilities to **know where each resident is** and to **have a way to tell families**.
- Neither requires a **live, shared roster** visible to responders or families.
- The failures (OIG, Tubbs, MonteCedro, Terraces) are exactly in tracking and in answering families.

## 7. Verdict

**Is "welfare checks + who can't evacuate" one well-evidenced problem that 911 and senior living share?**

- **Disaster side: yes, strongly.**
  - The dead are overwhelmingly older and mobility-limited: median age 77, at least a third mobility-impaired (LA 2025). Camp Fire mean age 72. Katrina 49% aged 75+.
  - Relatives' welfare and missing-person calls swamp responders and are mostly about people who are already safe:
    - LAPD: 160+ calls, "many" for people already evacuated, and no agency owned the queue.
    - Helene: 11,000 requests, about 1.4% urgent.
    - Maui: more than 3,000 found safe.
  - The few who truly can't leave are often known to someone (a caretaker, relative or facility) but to **no list a responder can use**. The Mitchells made three 911 calls, AccessLA was idle, and SNAP was scrapped.
- **Senior-living side: real but narrower.**
  - Facilities are legally required to track residents, know special needs and have a way to tell families (42 CFR 483.73; H&S §1569.695).
  - They still fail at exactly that: OIG found 19/24 plans lacked next-of-kin contacts and some residents were "lost"; MonteCedro and the Terraces left residents behind; more than 90% of LA plans were stale.
  - Families end up doing the welfare check: the Terraces "responsible party" called police, and Tubbs families evacuated residents themselves.
- **The everyday family-call load is plausible but has only vendor evidence.**

**Strongest 3-number chain for a pitch:**
1. **"77"**: median age of the people killed in the LA fires; at least 1 in 3 had mobility impairments. *(LA Times; AG average 77)*
2. **"11,000 → 150"**: welfare-check requests vs households actually needing an urgent check after Helene (about 1.4%). Locally: LAPD's **160+** Palisades evacuation calls, "many" for people already evacuated. *(828newsnow/Buncombe; LAPD AAR p.15)*
3. **"3 calls, 0 lists"**: the Mitchells called 911 three times saying "two disabled people in the house." The county had no registry (SNAP scrapped around 2016), and the paratransit service was ready but never called. In facilities, **19 of 24** nursing-home plans didn't send next-of-kin contacts with residents. *(LAist; LA Times; OIG)*

Optional fourth number: **36%** of LA seniors are LEP (ACS).

**What would contradict it:**
- **No published share of 911 disaster calls are welfare checks.** 160+ calls may be small next to LAPD's total, and the LAPD AAR doesn't give totals.
- **The LA deaths were in private homes, not senior facilities.** Facility residents were bused out, and the 2–3 left behind survived. A senior-living-only product would not have reached the Mitchells.
- **Citygate found no failure in Eaton alert timing.** The "nobody knew" story competes with "alerts were reasonable; resources were saturated." AccessLA not being called points to resource dispatch, not information.
- **Registry experts** (NCD, Cal OES, Kailes) argue lists without transport capacity give false assurance. Any "who can't leave" list must come with a transport handoff (paratransit, buses).
- **The everyday call load in senior living is unmeasured.** If staff don't feel phone burden, the daily use case weakens.
- **Privacy:** HIPAA 164.510(b) allows disaster disclosure, but RCFE residents and their families must consent to sharing mobility data.
