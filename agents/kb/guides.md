## Smoke or Wildfire Nearby, Not Yet Ordered to Evacuate
1. If your life or safety is in immediate danger, or you are trapped, call 911 and give your location.
2. Stay inside in a safe place, or go to a community building where there is less smoke.
3. Pick one room you can close off from outside air, and run a portable air cleaner or filter in it.
4. If you have an N95 mask, wear it to cut down how much smoke you breathe.
5. Watch for emergency alerts and air quality alerts.
6. Keep your go bag, with water and daily medicines, packed and ready.
7. If you feel unsafe or see fire coming toward you, leave. Don't wait for an official alert.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/wildfires, https://www.cdc.gov/wildfires/safety/index.html, https://lacounty.gov/2026/10/14/los-angeles-county-public-safety-agencies-urge-residents-to-prepare-for-high-winds-significant-fire-risk/

## Evacuation Order: Leaving Now
1. If you are trapped or in immediate danger, call 911 and give your location.
2. An evacuation order means leave right now. Don't wait.
3. Take your emergency kit, your medicines, important papers, and any medical supplies or mobility aids.
4. Close and lock your doors and windows.
5. Use the official evacuation routes, not shortcuts, because shortcuts may be blocked.
6. Leave a note saying when you left and where you are going.
7. Text family or friends to tell them where you are going. Save phone calls for emergencies.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/evacuation, https://www.ready.gov/wildfires, https://lacounty.gov/2026/10/14/los-angeles-county-public-safety-agencies-urge-residents-to-prepare-for-high-winds-significant-fire-risk/, https://lacounty.gov/emergency/

## Evacuating with a Disability, a Wheelchair, or No Way to Drive
1. If you are in immediate danger or can't get out, call 911.
2. If you need extra time or help, leave when an evacuation warning is issued. Don't wait for an order.
3. Call family, caregivers, or neighbors now and ask them to help you leave.
4. If you have no ride, call 211 to ask what help is available.
5. Pack your medicines, medical supplies, and mobility aids.
6. If you use a power wheelchair, take a lightweight manual chair as a backup if you can.
7. Know more than one place you can go for medical care, in case the first one is closed.
Sources: https://lacounty.gov/2026/10/14/los-angeles-county-public-safety-agencies-urge-residents-to-prepare-for-high-winds-significant-fire-risk/, https://lacounty.gov/emergency/, https://www.ready.gov/disability, https://www.ready.gov/evacuation

## Power Outage with Oxygen, a CPAP, or Other Powered Medical Equipment
1. If someone's life or safety is in immediate danger, call 911.
2. Switch to backup batteries or another backup power source for the device.
3. If you use a generator, keep it outdoors, away from doors and windows. Never run it inside or in a garage.
4. If it's very hot or very cold, go to a community or senior center that has power. Call 211 to find one.
5. Keep the refrigerator closed to hold the cold in.
6. If refrigerated medicine you depend on to live has been warm more than a day, call your doctor or pharmacist about it.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/power-outages, https://ready.lacounty.gov/power-outage/, https://lacounty.gov/2026/10/14/los-angeles-county-public-safety-agencies-urge-residents-to-prepare-for-high-winds-significant-fire-risk/

## Extreme Heat
1. Call 911 right away if someone may have heat stroke: very high body temperature, red, hot, dry skin with no sweat, a fast strong pulse, dizziness, confusion, or passing out.
2. If you don't have air conditioning, go to a cooling center. Call 211, any hour, to find the nearest one.
3. Drink plenty of fluids.
4. Take a cool shower or bath, and wear loose, light-colored clothing.
5. Check on older neighbors, family, and friends who don't have air conditioning.
6. Never leave a pet alone in a vehicle, even with the window open.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/heat, https://ready.lacounty.gov/heat/

## Earthquake: During and Right After
1. If someone is badly hurt after the shaking, call 911.
2. While it's shaking: drop to your hands and knees, cover your head and neck, and hold on until it stops.
3. In a wheelchair or walker: lock your wheels, cover your head and neck, and hold on.
4. If you're in bed, turn face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
5. Don't run outside or around your home while it's shaking.
6. After it stops, check yourself for injuries, and help others only if you're trained.
7. If you smell gas or the building is damaged, go outside and move away from it. Expect aftershocks, and drop, cover, and hold on again.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/earthquakes, https://ready.lacounty.gov/earthquakes/, https://www.cdc.gov/earthquakes/safety/stay-safe-after-an-earthquake.html, https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/index.html, https://lacounty.gov/emergency/

## Leaving with Pets During an Evacuation
1. If people are in immediate danger, call 911. People come first.
2. If you have pets or large animals, leave when an evacuation warning is issued, before an order.
3. Take your pets with you. Don't leave them behind.
4. Put each pet in a sturdy carrier or on a leash or harness.
5. Bring your pet's food, water, medicine, and ID records.
6. Many public shelters only allow service animals. Ask your local animal shelter or animal control office where you can take your pets.
Sources: https://www.ready.gov/pets, https://www.ready.gov/evacuation, https://lacounty.gov/emergency/

## Smell of Gas
1. Get everyone out of the building right away. Once you're safely away, call 911.
2. Don't turn lights on or off, light matches, smoke, or do anything that could make a spark.
3. If you can, open a window as you leave.
4. Call the gas company from outside or from a neighbor's home.
5. If you know how, and it's safe, turn off the gas at the outside main valve.
6. Never turn the gas back on yourself. Only a professional should do it.
7. Don't go back inside until officials say it's safe.
Sources: https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/index.html, https://www.ready.gov/safety-skills, https://www.cdc.gov/earthquakes/safety/stay-safe-after-an-earthquake.html
