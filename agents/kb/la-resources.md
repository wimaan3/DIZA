## Emergencies: 911
For police, fire, or medical emergencies anywhere in Los Angeles County, call 911. If you can't talk, stay on the line or text 911 where available.

## Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: 988
Call or text 988 any time, 24 hours a day, for suicidal thoughts, emotional distress, or a mental health or substance-use crisis. It's free and confidential.

## 211 LA County: social services
Dial 2-1-1 for help finding shelter, food, utility assistance, disaster aid and other health and human services, 24 hours a day. From outside LA County or a phone that can't dial 211, call 1-800-339-6993. Source: 211la.org.

## Elder and dependent adult abuse
To report abuse or neglect of an older adult (60+) or a dependent adult (18 to 59 with physical or mental impairments) in Los Angeles County, call the Elder and Dependent Adult Abuse Hotline at 1-877-477-3646 (1-877-4R-SENIORS), 24 hours a day. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911. Source: ad.lacounty.gov Adult Protective Services.

## Child abuse and neglect
To report a child in danger in Los Angeles County, call the DCFS Child Protection Hotline at 1-800-540-4000, 24 hours a day (from outside California: 213-639-4500; TDD: 1-800-272-6699). If a child is in immediate danger, call 911. Source: dcfs.lacounty.gov.

## Relay service: 711
Deaf, hard-of-hearing and speech-disabled callers can dial 711 for the California Relay Service.

## Live emergency information
Open shelters, evacuation orders and road closures change hourly. Ask the line, which checks current information, or check the county's official emergency pages. Never rely on old information for evacuation decisions; follow official orders.

## Go-bag basics
A go-bag should hold water, a few days of medication and copies of prescriptions, a phone charger, ID and important papers, a flashlight, a mask (N95 for smoke), glasses, and supplies for children and pets. People who use oxygen or powered medical equipment should plan for backup power and tell someone their plan.

## When to leave
If officials order an evacuation, leave right away. If you see fire or heavy smoke nearby, or feel unsafe, don't wait for an order. Tell a neighbor, and check on people who may need help leaving.

## Nearest public emergency department to USC
Los Angeles General Medical Center (formerly called LAC+USC Medical Center) has a 24/7 emergency department at 1983 Marengo St, Los Angeles 90033, about 5 miles northeast of the USC University Park Campus. For a life-threatening emergency, call 911 instead of driving. Source: dhs.lacounty.gov.

## Help after the Eaton fire
The LA County Department of Mental Health fire support line is 1-833-659-0600. The Altadena Community Center at 730 E Altadena Dr has county staff helping fire survivors with housing, tenant, consumer and worker protections, weekdays 8 AM to 5 PM. Source: lacounty.gov, dcba.lacounty.gov.
