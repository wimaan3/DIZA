## What this line is
The DIZA Community Line is a community and campus concerns line run by a USC student team for the InnovateLA hackathon (October 2026). An automated assistant answers, takes reports and requests, answers questions from approved information, and connects callers to people on the team. Calls are recorded for the student project.

## What this line is not
This line is not 911 and does not send police, fire, or ambulances. It cannot confirm that emergency services are coming. For any emergency, call 911. For thoughts of suicide or self-harm, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

## How callbacks work
If no person on the team is free, the line queues a callback. Callers get a short code (for example D-417) to check status later by calling back with the code and the last four digits of their phone. Callback times are estimates.

## Talking to a person
At any time, a caller can say "person" or press 0 to be connected to a person on the team or to get a callback. Pressing 1 means someone is hurt; the caller will be told to call 911 and offered a person.

## Volunteers
People can register to volunteer by phone. New volunteers are pending until a coordinator verifies them. Pending volunteers receive no tasks and no caller information.

## Privacy
The line collects only what a request needs. It never asks for Social Security numbers, student ID numbers, bank or card numbers. It does not share anyone's information, location or status with other callers. Callers can say "stop calling" to stop check-in calls, opt out of anonymous statistics, or ask for their data to be deleted. Recordings are kept for a short time for the student project.

## Check-in calls
Check-in calls are short, friendly, non-clinical calls to people who signed up. If someone signs up another person, calls start only after that person agrees on the first call. Saying "stop" ends the calls.

## Accessibility
Callers who are deaf, hard of hearing, or have a speech disability can use the keypad (0 for a person, 1 if someone is hurt) or the California Relay Service by dialing 711.
